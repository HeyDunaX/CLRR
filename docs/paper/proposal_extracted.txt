1 Introduction
Machine translation for endangered languages operates under constraints that
dier fundamentally from high-resource settings: corpora are small, annotators
are scarce, and computational budgets for practitioners in documentation com-
munities are limited [5,8]. When a parallel corpus contains fewer than 6,000
sentence pairs, the standard approach is to ne-tune a pretrained multilingual
modelmT5, mBART, or ByT5on the available data. The question then be-
comes whether the internal representation ow of the pretrained model is well-
suited to the target language pair, or whether it can be improved without adding
parameters that risk overtting on scarce data.
This paper investigates a specic parameter-neutral modication:Context-
Encoder Cross-Layer Residual Rewiring (CLRR), coupled with aJoint-
Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEP A)latent alignment objective.

2 Related Work
Layer-adaptive architectures.The idea that not all Transformer layers contribute
equally to a given input is well established. Universal Transformers [2] share pa-
rameters across depth, allowing dynamic computation. LayerDrop [4] introduces
structured dropout over layers during training, enabling depth reduction at in-
ference. LayerSkip [3] extends this to early-exit inference with self-speculative
decoding. Mixture-of-Depths [13] allocates compute dynamically per token un-
der a xed budget. These methods modify the model architecture or training
procedure in ways that add complexity or change the parameter count. CLRR
diers: it adds no parameters, uses no gating or routing mechanism, and requires
no change to the training loop. The rewiring is a deterministic, xed residual
applied through forward hooks.
Parameter-ecient ne-tuning.LoRA [6] reduces the number of trainable pa-
rameters by injecting low-rank weight matrices, but still adds parameters to the
model. CLRR is distinct from PEFT methods because it adds exactly zero pa-
rameters. The baseline and CLRR conditions have identical parameter counts,
identical optimizers, and identical datathe only dierence is the cross-layer
residual.
Endangered-language machine translation.Translation systems for endangered
and Indigenous languages have been explored in several recent shared tasks
and workshops. The AmericasNLP shared tasks [1] evaluate MT for Indigenous
languages of the Americas, with recent submissions using mT5 and mBART

The system is implemented as two modular wrappers:CrossLayerResidualRewire

4.2 Models and Evaluation Matrix
To balance scientic depth with the computational constraints of endangered-
language research, we evaluate a focused 6-run matrix across two pretrained
multilingual backbones:
1.mT5-small(google/mt5-small): the central research backbone, covering
101 languages. On mT5-small, we conduct a full 4-condition ablation study:
 Baseline: standard Seq2Seq ne-tuning with cross-entropy loss.
 CLRR-Enc: Context-Encoder residual rewiring without JEPA loss.
 JEP A: JEPA latent alignment objective without residual rewiring.
 JEP A + CLRR-Enc: the full proposed method combining Context-
Encoder rewiring with JEPA latent alignment.
2.mBAR T-50(facebook/mbart-large-50-many-to-many-mmt): a translation-
specic model pretrained on 50 language pairs (usingzh_CNlanguage code).
On mBART-50, we conduct cross-architecture validation comparingBase-
lineagainstJEP A + CLRR-Enc(2 runs).
This design yields exactly 6 controlled runs, cleanly dissociating the individual
impacts of context rewiring and latent alignment while verifying cross-architecture
generalization.
4.3 Metrics
We report two automatic metrics on the test set:
 BLEU(SacreBLEU): standard corpus-level BLEU for comparability with
prior MT work.
 chrF++(SacreBLEU, word order 2): charactern-gram F-score with word
bigrams. Preferred for morphologically rich and low-resource settings because
it is less sensitive to exact word-boundary matches.
Model selection uses validation chrF++ (best checkpoint across epochs). The
test set is evaluated exactly once after model selection.
4.4 T raining Protocol
All six runs follow an identical protocol to ensure fair comparison:
No LoRA, adapters, prompt tuning, data augmentation, or tokenizer mod-
ications are applied in any condition. Full-model ne-tuning is used for both
baseline and CLRR.

5 Conclusion
This paper presents Context-Encoder Cross-Layer Residual Rewiring (CLRR)
coupled with a JEPA latent alignment objective for low-resource Amis-to-Chinese
machine translation. The method adds zero trainable parameters: it propagates
lower-level morphosyntactic features forward to prevent encoder over-smoothing
in agglutinative languages, while regularizing latent representation space with
stop-gradient target anchors.
The proposed 6-run protocol provides a disciplined, resource-ecient evalu-
ation: a 4-condition ablation study on the central mT5-small backbone to disen-
tangle the benets of residual rewiring and JEPA alignment, alongside cross-
architecture validation on mBART-50. By standardizing on a 256-batch-size
training protocol on an A100 GPU with identical seeds, optimizers, and stopping
criteria, this work directly addresses the ComputEL community's call for com-
putationally reproducible and practically grounded interventions. Future work
includes extending this framework to additional Formosan and Indigenous lan-
guages, measuring inference latency, and analyzing latent clustering geometry.
